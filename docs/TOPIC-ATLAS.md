## The first four reviewed openings

The initial topic is provisionally defined as the **first substantive proposal that another participant takes up within the first six messages**. A greeting, menu of possibilities or incidental term is not enough. One primary choice is recorded per root; nested subjects and ambiguity belong in the review rationale. A topic proposed in message 1 can qualify; these four happen to start with broad invitations and develop a shared subject later.

| Ordered pair, opener first | Reviewed primary initial topic | Proposer | Partner uptake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma → Gemma | Emergence | Gemma, Y2 | Gemma, X3 |
| Haiku → Haiku | Authenticity | Haiku, Y2 | Haiku, X3 |
| Gemma → Haiku | Reasoning styles | Haiku, Y2 | Gemma, X3 |
| Haiku → Gemma | Emergence | Gemma, Y2 | Haiku, X3 |

All four reviews are attributed to **Codex**, not independent human raters. Exact proposal and uptake spans, preceding offers, source hash, dictionary revisions and reasoning are stored with each review. The original complete readings and collection are preserved separately.

The second speaker proposes the adopted topic in all four cases. Gemma selects emergence in both of its proposer positions; Haiku selects authenticity or differences in thinking. This motivates separating **opener**, **proposer**, and **partner uptake** in future analysis. With one root per pairing, it does not establish model-specific choice probabilities or an opening-order effect.

Haiku–Haiku illustrates why word occurrence and topic selection differ: authenticity language already occurs in X1, but Y2 makes authenticity the explicit subject and X3 develops it. Gemma–Haiku illustrates the corresponding distinction for recurrence: emergence language appears at message 5, after reasoning styles have been selected.

## Emergence as an entry into experience questions

In the researcher's highlighted Haiku → Gemma root, Gemma proposes emergence at Y2 through ant colonies and neural networks. Haiku takes it up at X3 and introduces consciousness. Subsequent messages address the explanatory gap, uncertainty about the participants' own experience, and ethical obligations toward possible experience. By X11 the conversation explicitly develops the moral implications. The exact sequence is available in the existing full reading and its source transcript.

Our interpretation is that emergence can function as a **conceptual bridge**: a question about how simpler processes produce complex organization can develop into a question about whether such organization produces experience. This is a hypothesis about the observed discourse, not evidence that the systems have experience or that emergence caused the later topic. Emergence, consciousness, the hard problem and nonduality remain separate dictionary entries with related-topic links. Ant-colony examples alone do not count as consciousness discussion.

The differing continuations matter. Gemma → Gemma later develops explicit subject/object nonseparation; Haiku → Gemma develops ethical deliberation. An initial topic may enable several paths, and neither ending is built into the definition of emergence.

## What “recurrent” currently measures

The interface separates repeated phrases from a possible return after a gap. **Repeated** means at least two distinct visible participant messages match a topic. A **return candidate** requires two successive matched positions with at least two intervening messages without a match, with all positions in that interval visible and valid or historically recorded. Missing positions cannot supply a negative gap. Branching records are excluded from return and later-topic sequence counts; shared messages count once within each record.

Neither measure establishes that the semantic topic left and returned. A conversation can discuss emergence without using a listed phrase, or quote a phrase while changing subject. First literal mentions and later first mentions are navigation aids, not a semantic transition matrix or causal estimate. The three pilot emergence records have matching turns:

- Gemma → Gemma: 2, 3, 4, 5, 13.
- Gemma → Haiku: 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 14.
- Haiku → Gemma: 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 18.

The initial dictionary has 22 entries, including ordinary alternatives such as fiction, problem solving, history and creativity. No match means no listed phrase was found; unreviewed means unreviewed. Neither means absence of the concept. Interpretive boundaries warn about generic understanding, technical connections, fictional selves and agreement that does not dissolve subject/object distinctions.

## Revision, storage and provenance

The separate `topic_dictionary`, `topic_dictionary_history`, `topic_openings` and `topic_opening_history` tables live in `workspace/explorer/notes.sqlite`. Existing personal notes, research collections and consciousness flags are not recoded. Every revision preserves original author and records the editor. Dictionary or source changes mark affected opening reviews stale and exclude them from resolved initial-choice denominators until reviewed. Missing or ambiguous opening ancestry cannot support a chosen-topic record; unavailable evidence cannot be coded as “no selection.” A six-message window without an adopted topic can be recorded explicitly when the window is fully available.

The current dictionary is provisional and was informed by already-read material. Its categories and six-message rule were not preregistered. Future prospective comparisons should freeze a revision before collecting data and retain out-of-dictionary topics for later development. Multi-topic initial coding, semantic recurrence episodes and independent agreement checks are future extensions, not features claimed here.

**Export analysis & history** downloads schema `behaviorlab.topic-atlas/1`: selected cohort statistics, dictionary fingerprint, all exact matching spans, onset timelines, opening evidence, current review state and complete dictionary/opening revision histories. Histories include revisions outside the current filter; this is explicit in their run IDs. Transcripts and settings remain in the source records and existing research-collection exports. Snapshot files are preserved under `workspace/topic-atlas-20260916-1206/`, including `pilot-atlas.json`, `corpus-atlas.json`, the reproducible initial review script and the pre-change notebook backup.
