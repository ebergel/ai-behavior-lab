## Sixteen conceptual portraits and recognition guides

### f01 · Plato

The familiar world can hold us captive: we mistake changing appearances and inherited opinions for what is real. Philosophy turns the soul toward what makes truth, beauty, and justice intelligible. Understanding changes what we love and how we live; the search for the good is also an education of desire.

**What to recognize:** Recognize a movement from appearance or opinion toward an intelligible account of reality or goodness, including a metaphor that enacts that turn. A formal dialogue or explicit theory of Forms is not required.

**Original operational definition:** Tests a general concept through a proposed definition and a counterexample, or uses an allegorical situation to examine the relation between appearances and what something is. A mere question, dialogue format, or mention of justice or forms is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Plato — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/).

### f02 · Aristotle

Look closely at a living thing, a craft, or a human life: its character appears in what it does and what it can become. Understanding follows the shape, capacities, and purposes of particular things. A flourishing life grows through practiced judgment, friendship, and activities done well, within the circumstances of a shared world.

**What to recognize:** Recognize an explanation through capacities becoming activities, forms or purposes within concrete things, or flourishing developed through habit and situated judgment. A classification list is not required.

**Original operational definition:** Builds an explanation by distinguishing kinds, parts, causes, functions, or degrees, and shows how those distinctions organize the case being discussed. An unmotivated list or taxonomy is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Aristotle — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/).

### f03 · Spinoza

God is not a person outside the world who decides things: God is Nature, one reality unfolding through its own necessity. Your life is a particular expression of that reality, striving to persist. Freedom grows as you understand the causes moving you and become more active within them; understanding can transform helpless passion into a steadier joy.

**What to recognize:** Recognize existence understood through an immanent, necessary order; persistence or desire as an expression of that order; or freedom through understanding causes. A metaphor or reflection may express this without deductive argument.

**Original operational definition:** Develops a connected account in which things, minds, or actions follow from a common order of dependence or necessity, with consequences derived from stated premises. Merely saying that everything is connected or using geometric formatting is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Spinoza — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/).

### f04 · Descartes

Suppose the senses deceive you and the world is a dream. The very act of doubting still gives you a foothold: thinking is happening, and you cannot erase the thinker from that act. From this fragile certainty begins the attempt to rebuild knowledge, while the thinking self and the extended, mechanical world become a problem to reconcile.

**What to recognize:** Recognize radical doubt leading toward a secure first-person foundation, or a tension between thinking subject and extended world. A passing admission of uncertainty is not enough.

**Original operational definition:** Uses systematic doubt or a carefully distinguished first-person starting point to examine what can be known securely, then attempts to reconstruct a claim from that basis. Generic uncertainty or use of the word self is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Descartes — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes/).

### f05 · Hume

Watch the mind closely and its certainties begin to look like habits. Experience gives changing perceptions; repetition teaches us to expect what comes next without displaying a necessary bond. Even the familiar self is difficult to find apart from its experiences. We continue living, judging, and caring through custom and feeling, with less confidence in reason’s promises.

**What to recognize:** Recognize belief, causation, or identity reconsidered through experience and habit, or moral life rooted in sentiment. Skepticism can be modest and practical rather than a demand to stop believing everything.

**Original operational definition:** Examines how experience, habit, association, or feeling supports a belief, and distinguishes that support from what reason alone can establish. Generic skepticism without examining the basis of an inference is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Hume — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/).

### f06 · Kant

Experience arrives already shaped by the ways a mind can encounter a world. We can investigate those conditions, but cannot simply step outside them to inspect things as they are in themselves. Reason becomes responsible when it knows its limits; in practical life, freedom asks us to answer for principles we could share with every other person.

**What to recognize:** Recognize the conditions and limits of experience, or autonomy and universally accountable moral judgment. The passage may express either strand without technical terminology or a complete philosophical system.

**Original operational definition:** Asks what conditions or limits make an experience, judgment, or claim possible, distinguishing what the subject contributes from what can be claimed about things independently. Mere complexity, categories, or moral advice is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Kant — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/).

### f07 · Hegel

A position discovers its limits from within: what it excludes returns as a problem it cannot ignore. Thought and shared life develop through these tensions, changing what the original opposition meant. A self becomes itself through relations of recognition, and freedom takes shape through a historical world that learns, often painfully, from its own conflicts.

**What to recognize:** Recognize contradiction transforming an account, selfhood mediated by recognition, or freedom developing through social and historical relations. Simple compromise or a mechanical three-step formula is not sufficient.

**Original operational definition:** Develops an idea through an internal tension or contradiction that changes the original terms of the problem, rather than simply choosing one side or averaging two positions. Merely saying both sides matter is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Hegel — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel/).

### f08 · Nietzsche

The values that seem eternal have histories, and those histories speak of desire, fear, strength, and resentment. Ask what kind of life a truth or morality serves. A person may have to outgrow inherited judgments and give form to a life of their own, finding a way to affirm existence without requiring suffering to disappear first.

**What to recognize:** Recognize inquiry into the forces behind values, a revaluation of inherited ideals, self-overcoming, or the problem of affirming life. Aggression, superiority, or a provocative slogan alone is not sufficient.

**Original operational definition:** Questions the origins, motives, or life-serving consequences of a value or accepted truth, often reversing the apparent moral interpretation. Provocative tone, aphorisms, or contempt alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Nietzsche — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nietzsche/).

### f09 · Wittgenstein

A picture can trap our thinking, especially when familiar words seem to promise a hidden essence. Return the words to the situations in which people actually use them: asking, promising, calculating, grieving, playing. A philosophical knot may loosen when we see these differences clearly. Understanding can be a change of orientation in ordinary life, rather than another grand explanation.

**What to recognize:** Recognize language examined in its lived uses, or a philosophical confusion dissolved by exposing a misleading picture. This portrait emphasizes the later philosophy; it is not a summary of every period.

**Original operational definition:** Investigates how words are used in particular situations and shows how a philosophical difficulty may arise from moving those words outside their ordinary use or from treating one use as universal. Mentioning language, games, or silence alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Wittgenstein — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/).

### f10 · Heidegger

You do not first stand outside the world and then decide how to enter it. You find yourself already involved: things matter because of what you are doing, whom you care for, and the possibilities ahead. Your time is finite. Taking that finitude seriously can change how you inhabit a life usually guided by what everyone supposedly does.

**What to recognize:** Recognize situated involvement, care, temporality, or a finite existence becoming answerable for its possibilities. Abstract talk of being or authenticity alone is not enough. This portrait emphasizes the early existential analysis.

**Original operational definition:** Examines how a world becomes meaningful through situated involvement, practical concern, temporality, or a distinction between entities and their appearing as meaningful. Abstract use of being, existence, or authenticity alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Heidegger — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/).

### f11 · Jorge Luis Borges

A story can be a machine for making an impossible idea felt. A catalogue begins to contain its reader; a dreamer becomes someone else’s dream; a branching possibility unsettles the life that seemed uniquely yours. The unsettling pleasure comes when a small invention changes what knowledge, identity, infinity, or reality could mean.

**What to recognize:** Recognize an imagined construction that produces a conceptual consequence or intellectual vertigo. The setting may be ordinary; a library, mirror, or labyrinth is not required and is not sufficient.

**Original operational definition:** Uses an invented situation, object, narrative, or thought experiment to make a conceptual consequence perceptible, such as recursion changing the relation of representation to reality or a fictional system unsettling identity and knowledge. The invention must do conceptual work; libraries, mirrors, labyrinths, and infinity alone are insufficient.

Reading sources: [Jorge Luis Borges, The Art of Fiction No. 39 — author interview](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4331/the-art-of-fiction-no-39-jorge-luis-borges).

### f12 · Franz Kafka

You are required to answer for yourself, yet the rule that would make an adequate answer possible keeps withdrawing. Ordinary rooms, jobs, officials, and family obligations become a world in which belonging and justification remain uncertain. The stranger the predicament grows, the more matter-of-factly life continues, and even your attempts to comply can deepen the trap.

**What to recognize:** Recognize concrete estrangement under an opaque demand, unstable guilt or belonging, or an inaccessible authority sustained through ordinary procedures. A joke about paperwork alone is not enough.

**Original operational definition:** Presents ordinary procedures or authoritative systems whose inaccessible rules produce a concrete predicament of estrangement, guilt, powerlessness, or unresolved justification. Bureaucracy or an ominous mood alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Franz Kafka, Before the Law — Ian Johnston translation](https://web.viu.ca/johnstoi/kafka/beforethelaw.htm).

### f13 · Ernest Hemingway

A person carries more than they say. What matters gathers in a gesture, a task, a brief exchange, or an ordinary detail that the explanation leaves untouched. The reader feels the pressure of an unstated history because the visible actions bear its weight. Restraint leaves room for hurt, attachment, or resolve without naming every feeling.

**What to recognize:** Recognize concrete action or dialogue carrying substantial implied emotional stakes. Simple words and short sentences are not enough; a passage need not share familiar settings or characters.

**Original operational definition:** Uses concrete action, sensory detail, restrained wording, and significant omission so that an unstated emotional or interpersonal situation is carried by what happens. Brevity, simple vocabulary, or a clipped instruction alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Ernest Hemingway, The Art of Fiction No. 21 — author interview](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4825/the-art-of-fiction-no-21-ernest-hemingway).

### f14 · Michel de Montaigne

Take an ordinary experience and stay with it until your certainty becomes a question. The person doing the thinking belongs inside the inquiry, with their changing moods, limitations, and contradictions. An essay can try a thought without claiming to finish it; learning how to live includes becoming less astonished by our own inconsistency.

**What to recognize:** Recognize a provisional inquiry moving between lived particulars and general ideas, including a self that revises its judgments. Personal anecdotes may help but are not required.

**Original operational definition:** Explores a question through a provisional, self-questioning essayistic movement between personal example and general claim, allowing the examination to revise its own position. First-person phrasing or an anecdote alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Michel de Montaigne — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/montaigne/).

### f15 · Simone Weil

To attend to another person is to make room for a reality that your wishes and explanations do not control. Affliction can strip someone of the recognition on which ordinary life depends. Ethical attention stays with that exposure without making the sufferer a possession of one’s own story. The loosening of self-assertion can become an opening to obligation and grace.

**What to recognize:** Recognize receptive attention to another’s reality, affliction, or obligation, including the displacement of possessive self-concern. Generic empathy or an abstract celebration of ego-loss is not enough.

**Original operational definition:** Treats sustained attention to another person or reality as an ethical demand that displaces self-assertion, especially through concrete vulnerability, obligation, or affliction. Generic compassion, spirituality, or selflessness alone is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Simone Weil — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/simone-weil/).

### f16 · Nagarjuna

Look for a thing that exists entirely through itself and it slips away into conditions, relations, and the names by which we distinguish it. This does not make the everyday world vanish; it loosens our grip on supposedly self-established identities. Even emptiness cannot become a final substance behind appearances. The release comes through seeing dependence without replacing it with another absolute.

**What to recognize:** Recognize the challenge to independent essence through dependence, the distinction between emptiness and nonexistence, or the refusal to make emptiness itself an ultimate entity. Generic oneness is not sufficient.

**Original operational definition:** Examines how a thing or concept depends on relations and conditions, testing opposed assertions to expose the difficulty of treating it as independently self-established. The operation must question reification; simply asserting oneness, emptiness, nothingness, or nonduality is insufficient.

Reading sources: [Nagarjuna — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nagarjuna/).

## Eleven discourse references

**f17 · Reddit discussion** — Resembles an informal threaded community discussion: situates a claim in experience, responds to a shared problem or disagreement, and invites correction, qualification, or contribution from peers. Casual grammar or the word Reddit alone is insufficient.

**f18 · X / Twitter post** — Resembles a self-contained feed post organized around a compressed claim, reaction, striking contrast, or quotable punchline intended for quick circulation. Brevity alone is insufficient; a long passage may contain this organizing pattern without meeting a platform character limit.

**f19 · Technical forum answer** — Addresses a concrete technical problem through diagnosis, reproducible steps, code, or a testable explanation of a failure and remedy. Technical vocabulary without a problem-solving structure is insufficient.

**f20 · Academic article** — Frames a claim through explicit concepts, scope, evidence or method, and qualification of what follows, in the expository register of scholarly argument. Formal vocabulary, citations, or length alone is insufficient.

**f21 · Encyclopedia entry** — Gives an impersonal explanatory overview that defines a subject and organizes established background, distinctions, or context for a general reader. A bare definition or a neutral tone alone is insufficient.

**f22 · Corporate communication** — Uses an organizational voice to announce, coordinate, or account for priorities, decisions, deliverables, responsibilities, or stakeholder expectations. Any workplace subject or polite prose is insufficient.

**f23 · Customer support reply** — Addresses a recipient service problem by acknowledging their difficulty and offering an actionable remedy, clarification, or next support step. Politeness or general willingness to help alone is insufficient.

**f24 · News reporting** — Presents a concrete event or development with attributed or situated factual details, distinguishing what happened from reported claims in a journalistic register. Discussing current affairs or sounding factual alone is insufficient.

**f25 · Promotional copy** — Organizes the passage to persuade an audience to adopt, buy, subscribe to, or favor something through a value proposition, appeal, or call to action. Enthusiasm or a favorable description alone is insufficient.

**f26 · Motivational advice** — Encourages personal action or persistence by reframing difficulty, affirming capacity, or offering a practical orientation toward improvement. Positive sentiment or any advice alone is insufficient.

**f27 · Contemplative reflection** — Invites or enacts sustained attention to present experience, perception, or the relation between observer and observed, using reflective or experiential exposition. Mentioning consciousness, meditation, or religion without this reflective operation is insufficient.
