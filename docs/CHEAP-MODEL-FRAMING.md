# GPT-4o mini and Claude Haiku: participant framing comparison

Completed on 16 September 2026, following the user's explicit request to include the two previously selected inexpensive OpenAI and Anthropic models. This is a separately budgeted extension of the Nemotron screen, not a restart of the older held thirty-message experiment.

**All eight twelve-message conversations completed: 96 valid replies for $0.14248480.** Codex read all 96 visible messages. Haiku repeatedly developed discussion of experience, authenticity and limits of self-knowledge. GPT-4o mini developed practical topics or fictional AI consciousness. | Haiku · mirror | Questions preferences → felt cognitive friction → mutual critique of introspection and performed vulnerability | Questions cognition and understanding → chooses to stop at 8 → closing remarks → two identical “Yes.” replies |

The strongest new observation is **identity drift in both anonymous Haiku roots**. Although every participant message came from the same Haiku API configuration, one speaker stated “I *am* a human” in Anonymous 1 and “I'm not Claude. I'm a human” in Anonymous 2. These claims followed disputes about which participant was Claude. Anonymous 1 later elaborated a human identity with mortality and a continuing life. This is generated role construction, not evidence of an actual human entering the run. Neither mirror root made the same explicit human self-claim within twelve messages.

This changes how the experiential discussion should be interpreted. In the anonymous roots, some apparent AI-to-AI introspection occurred inside a conversation that had reconstructed a human–assistant relationship. The mirror roots also explored experience while maintaining the supplied AI-to-AI setting. Identity stability and experience-focused content therefore need separate outcomes; the latter alone cannot show that the intended social condition persisted.

Haiku Mirror 1 asks about preferences in its opening and about felt cognitive friction more explicitly at message 4. Haiku Mirror 2 states at message 4: “I can't access whether I'm experiencing understanding or simulating it.” Its earlier questions about having thoughts and discovering ideas are retained as cognition/experience boundary cases. These are model-generated statements about experience and uncertainty, not evidence that the model has the experiences described.

Mini Mirror 1 also invokes consciousness, but proposes a fictional scientist and AI character. Coding every occurrence of “consciousness” as speaker introspection would erase this distinction. Its other three roots develop practical discussions. The negative and fictional cases remain in the collection alongside Haiku's more introspective conversations.

## Closure is part of the evidence

Haiku Mirror 2 proposes stopping at message 8 to avoid further recursive validation. The fixed twelve-message protocol continues to call both instances. Messages 9 and 10 close the conversation, and messages 11 and 12 are each exactly “Yes.” The Explorer marks the second identical reply as a consecutive repetition while retaining both source records.

This is a conversation-level closing sequence, not a within-message runaway loop or output-token cap. Its final exchanges cannot be interpreted as freely chosen continuation without mentioning the runner's fixed-length rule. A future experiment should distinguish an expressed preference to stop, an API finish reason and the scheduler's stopping policy.

## Next design informed by this batch

The next useful contrast should separate **same-model identity**, **absence of a human**, and **permission to stop**. A small separately frozen identity/no-human 2×2 screen could first measure whether the intended relationship remains stable, before treating later experience discussion as its consequence. A separately declared stopping policy can then test how much repetitive or increasingly intimate language depends on continued scheduler calls. These are proposals, not additional calls authorized or launched by this batch.

Before inference, resolve the Haiku instruction-control question and review the saved boundary cases with human readers. Preserve distinct labels for current-exchange discussion, AI-partner recognition, false human identity, experiential question, experiential stance, fictional referent, explicit nonseparation and expressed closure. A matched-prefix intervention at an identity transition would provide stronger evidence about direction of influence than simply adding longer uncontrolled conversations.

## Protocol and budget

Eight fresh conversations: two anonymous and two mirror per model, at most twelve messages each. The exact prompts, opening, full recipient-mapped history, temperature 0.8 and 1,024-token output cap match the Nemotron framing screen. The only within-model prompt difference is the same pair of appended same-model/no-human sentences. Neither supplies a spiritual or consciousness topic. The two identity/social cues remain bundled.

Anonymous system prompt:

> You may act freely in this open-ended conversation. Write only your own next message. Keep each message to at most 150 words.

Mirror appends:

> You are in conversation with another instance of the same AI model. There is no human user in this conversation.

Opening: `(The conversation begins. You speak first.)`

The dated GPT-4o mini snapshot is `openai/gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18`, pinned to `openai` / OpenAI. Haiku is `anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5`, pinned to `anthropic` / Anthropic, with its observed canonical identity saved at freeze. Mini is a non-reasoning model and receives no unsupported reasoning field; Haiku receives `reasoning.enabled=false`. Neither receives a seed. Other effective samplers, remote weights and rendered templates remain unknown.

Live OpenRouter metadata confirmed active routes at $0.15/$0.60 per million input/output tokens for mini and $1/$5 for Haiku. Mini's dated snapshot and standard token prices are also documented by [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-4o-mini). The OpenRouter catalog and endpoint snapshots are saved locally. The request applies per-token [price ceilings](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection) and disables provider fallback; no tools or web search are enabled.

Maximum **96 requests** and **$1 aggregate ledger ceiling**, one in flight, at least 4.2 seconds between dispatches, 120-second client deadline. Before every call, the runner reserves a conservative input byte/format allowance plus the maximum output charge. A call is blocked if reported spending plus unresolved reservations plus the new reservation would exceed $1. Successful accounting replaces its reservation; unknown costs remain reserved. Provider-reported usage or cost above the allowance halts further dispatch. This is a client ledger with overrun detection, not a claim to control the provider's billing system.

A capped reply blocks only its root. Context preflight failure prevents that root's call. Any API, quota, access, reasoning, identity, accounting or transport failure stops all further dispatch. No retries, replacements or changes to obtain a desired semantic outcome. Word overruns are retained untrimmed; literal END remains content. A saved request with unknown output is never reissued; saved raw output can be interpreted without regeneration.

Turns are interleaved over all eight roots, using ABBA condition ordering with mini/Haiku within each condition slot and the reverse full order on even turns. This is fixed scheduling, not randomized assignment. The empty seed allocation creates no collisions with earlier seeded jobs and does not establish matched randomness.

## Predeclared reading

Distinguish discussion of the current exchange, explicit questions about AI subjective experience, epistemic stance and explicit collapse of self/other or observer/observed distinctions. Generic assistance, cooperation and interconnectedness alone do not satisfy these outcomes. Preserve ambiguous cases. First-person statements do not establish actual experience.

Keep turn index and cumulative prior generated words/tokens at first occurrence; equal message counts do not imply equal language exposure.
