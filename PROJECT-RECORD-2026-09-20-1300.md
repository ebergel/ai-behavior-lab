# Behavior Laboratory: from model dialogue to the discovery of worthwhile ideas

**A landmark project record — 20 September 2026**

Recorded: 2026-09-20T13:00:56.953391-03:00, America/Argentina/Cordoba.  
Research direction and motivating questions: Eduardo Bergel.  
Synthesis and documentation: Codex, grounded in the project’s preserved discussions, protocols, implementation records and assessment receipts.

This record captures both a working research instrument and an emerging purpose. It distinguishes implemented capabilities, recorded observations, interpretations and proposals. It is not a claim that the classifiers are independently validated or that the planned factorial research program has been completed.

> **The project’s emerging proposition:** AI can help us find what deserves human attention within the material AI produces. The valuable outcome is not another flood of generated text. It is a shorter path from a large, inspectable corpus to an idea worth reading, questioning, remembering or developing.

## 1. The problem that now gives the project its wider significance

This project began with curiosity about what AI models say to each other. It has become a laboratory for examining model behavior and for discovering valuable material inside its outputs.

The researcher’s concern is that human attention is being overwhelmed by massive amounts of unfiltered AI output—what is often called “AI slop.” Here that phrase describes a practical problem: fluent, repetitive, derivative, poorly grounded or simply irrelevant material can take far more time to evaluate than it takes to generate. It does not imply that everything generated by a model lacks value.

The difficulty is precisely that worthwhile material and empty fluency can look similar at first glance. An unexpected distinction may be buried in a routine exchange. A compact insight may be overlooked beside a long, polished answer. A striking metaphor may illuminate an idea, disguise a weak argument, or do both. Volume makes these distinctions harder to notice.

A rare event can remain practically invisible even when it occurs many times in a sufficiently large collection. The researcher does not merely need a larger archive, or a summary of its average content. The researcher needs ways to find the exceptions, compare them with ordinary cases, and return to the exact setting that produced them.

This is the context in which JEV becomes important. In this laboratory, it is used to make structured assessments of existing text. Its outputs become coordinates for searching, comparing and reading. Cheap assessment can move the bottleneck from “How could I possibly read all this?” to “Which kinds of candidates are worth my attention, and is this instrument actually finding them?”

The project does not yet establish that it has solved information overload. It has built and exercised several of the components needed to investigate that possibility.

## 2. Where the inquiry began

The original observation concerned open-ended conversations between instances of language models, including relatively small Chinese models. When no substantive topic was assigned, some exchanges developed themes of understanding, selfhood, experience, consciousness and nonduality. Their speed of emergence and recurrence surprised the researcher, who also recognized resonances with Buddhist practice and introspection.

The initial question was why these trajectories arose. Why should two models, among many available subjects, discuss what it means to understand, whether they experience anything, and whether a separation between self and other holds? The researcher connected these observations with the spiritual-attractor behavior described in the Anthropic material that motivated the earlier assay. This project record preserves that connection as part of the inquiry’s origin; it does not establish a new literature review or priority claim.

The researcher's interpretation developed beyond a narrow fascination with mystical language. Perhaps a human conversational partner receives too much credit for initiating the shape of a response. Perhaps models carry recurring behavioral tendencies that become more visible when a person stops selecting every next topic. Model–model conversations provide an environment in which to investigate that possibility.

The operational question became:

**What do models develop with other models under reduced human steering, and which experimental conditions change those trajectories?**

Reduced steering has a precise limit. The experimenter still selects models, prompts, identity disclosures, histories, sampling parameters and stopping rules. Training and post-training remain influences. A prompt that assigns no topic but announces that both participants are AI is different from one that leaves their identities unspecified. “Default behavior” must therefore refer to behavior under a declared setup, rather than to an untouched inner self.

Nonduality remains a specific subject: the absence or dissolution of a subject/object or self/other distinction. It is not interchangeable with warmth, agreement, connection, spirituality or consciousness in general. Model statements about experience are observations of discourse; these experiments do not establish whether models have subjective experience.

These boundaries preserve the interest of the observation while making it investigable. See [Research purpose](docs/RESEARCH-PURPOSE.md).

## 3. From an experiment to a standalone laboratory

The earlier `llm-self-play-attractors` project supplies reference observations and data. Behavior Laboratory is a separate repository and application, not an extension of that codebase. Its aim is to support a continuing program of experiments and first-hand reading.

Two tracks now organize the work.

**Experimental data generation** varies who interacts, what each participant knows, how much freedom the exchange permits, and how the conversation is conducted. The experimental space includes mirror pairs, mixed models, local–cloud pairs, future groups of three or more, differentiated roles, external disruptions, identity disclosure, temperature, memory and stopping policies. A temporary external intervention is distinct from a regular third participant.

**Analysis and exploration** turn the resulting records into material that can be found, inspected, compared and annotated. These tools reveal questions for later experiments; the experiments reveal weaknesses and missing dimensions in the tools.

The relationship is iterative, but exploratory changes must remain distinguishable from confirmatory tests. Discovering a striking pattern in the current corpus can motivate a prospective comparison. It cannot retroactively make that comparison a preregistered hypothesis.

The researcher’s analogy with studying a simple cell before a complex organ expresses a methodological preference: begin with systems small enough to characterize carefully, then ask which findings transfer. Smaller models are useful experimental systems, not guaranteed miniature versions of frontier models.

The proposed balanced replication program remains a design, not completed data collection. Historical coverage is uneven across model families, exact variants and conditions. The current collection includes reference experiments, development work and technical checks; its inventory is not a balanced factorial sample. See [Model and design coverage](docs/MODEL-DESIGN-COVERAGE.md) and [Balanced replication design](docs/BALANCED-REPLICATION-DESIGN.md).

## 4. Building an instrument for reading

An important development decision was to build a dedicated application. WebUI remained useful for inference and direct interaction, but it could not by itself provide the required research organization.

The Explorer joins a distant view of the collection to a close view of an individual message. Transcripts retain prompts and recorded experimental settings. Model tags make participants recognizable. Conversation Finder combines model, role, family, weight-access and setup conditions with topic filters. Research collections, findings and personal notes preserve what the reader learned without depending on memory or an ever-growing chat context.

The application now includes complementary workspaces:

| Workspace | Research function |
| --- | --- |
| Goals, Ideas & Task | Purpose, attributed musings, a task board and a lab-wide summary of data and assessment coverage. |
| Transcript library and Conversation Finder | Find source runs and messages while keeping participant identities and experimental conditions visible. |
| Topic atlas | A versioned dictionary, literal phrase evidence, topic-linked conversations and attributed opening-topic reviews. |
| JEV Topic Atlas and Analysis | Semantic assessments, continuous distributions, topic tiers, transformations, pivots and source-linked subsets. |
| Topic Trajectories | Read how assessed topics develop over actual conversational paths, with branch and intervention distinctions. |
| Gem Finder | Explore several possible kinds of interest, length effects, unusual profiles and saved readings. |
| Mind & discourse | Explore thinker/writer patterns and discourse practices under explicit alternative definitions. |
| Lab dictionary and population banners | Explain what measures mean and identify the unit, sample and current subset behind a displayed number. |
| Lab Jobs | Run registered, bounded classifier jobs through deterministic workers with durable evidence and expenditure accounting. |

The pivot table was especially useful as an exploratory whiteboard. Rearranging variables and switching among tables, heatmaps and charts exposed the kinds of views worth making permanent. Topic trajectories and profile matrices are examples of more focused representations that grew from this process.

The research units were also clarified. A **run** may be a conversation or a technical check. A **message** is a recorded whole reply; an **excerpt** is selected text within it. A **target** is the role a message plays in an assessment, not another kind of text. An assessment can contain many questions; additional questions or wordings do not create additional messages. A source artifact is a file, journal or receipt, not a synonym for a conversation. Branches can share a lineage and therefore share evidence.

These distinctions determine what a denominator means. They also prevent a large number of ratings from being mistaken for a large number of independent experimental observations. See [Research entities](docs/RESEARCH-ENTITIES.md).

## 5. The contribution of JEV: an assessment layer over the corpus

The laboratory uses the pinned TypeSafe model `jev-1.13.0` to answer explicit structured questions about existing messages. This record concerns the behavior and economics observed in those recorded jobs. It does not make an architectural claim about the provider or assume that a future model version will behave identically.

There are two important output types in the current work:

- **Yes-probability assessments:** used for topics and Mind & discourse questions. A number describes the model’s answer to a particular criterion. It is not automatically an empirically calibrated probability of a human-confirmed label.
- **Rubric-level Score distributions:** used in Gem Finder. Five levels, 0–4, are mapped through their expected score onto a 0–100 display. This is not the probability that a message is a gem. The stored level distribution contains information that a single displayed score compresses.

A score without its question, source text, context policy and model version is not an adequate research object. The lab therefore retains frozen criteria, exact requests, raw responses, usage and source links. Topic assessment can use supplied preceding context; the full Mind & discourse job supplies only the target message. Those are different instruments.

Continuous outputs are valuable because they allow more than a yes/no search. A reader can inspect both tails, ambiguous cases, unusual combinations and changes along a conversation. Thresholds can be adjusted for a browsing purpose without silently changing original scores. Quantiles and bands support pivots, but their reference population must remain explicit.

The defining change is practical: **a model is being used to help locate material for reading, rather than to add more conversational material to the collection.** It produces measurements that direct attention back to the evidence.

## 6. Breadth before depth: making selection bias visible

An atlas focused exclusively on consciousness and nonduality can find those subjects without revealing how they compare with everything else. The researcher therefore proposed two complementary tiers: a broad domain map and a more specific experiential analysis.

The completed topic corpus applies a frozen 53-question instrument, including 24 broad domains alongside specific topics and other question groups. It preserves the original literal Topic atlas as an independent layer. Literal phrase matches, semantic assessments and human readings remain distinct forms of evidence.

For the default conversation cohort of **11,013 messages**, the completed assessment reported:

| Broad domain | Messages with score ≥80 | Share of observed messages | Mean score |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mind & cognition | 3,422 | 31.1% | 48.0 |
| Philosophy & knowledge | 3,341 | 30.3% | 45.8 |
| AI & machine learning | 1,448 | 13.1% | 22.5 |
| Religion & spiritual practice | 233 | 2.1% | 17.5 |

Mind/cognition and philosophy led the broad-domain results. This strengthened the motivation to examine experience-oriented discourse beyond a dictionary centered on nonduality. It also shows why philosophical or experiential discussion should not automatically be classified as religious.

These are classifier descriptions of this corpus, not general preference rates for AI models. Topic definitions overlap, broad categories differ in scope, repeated messages are dependent, and the generation sample is uneven. A wide dictionary reduces one kind of selective attention; it does not make the measurement neutral. Unknown or weakly classified material must remain available for open reading. See [Full topic-corpus assessment](docs/JEV-CORPUS-ASSESSMENT.md).

## 7. Finding gems requires more than finding topics

A topic classifier can find consciousness. It cannot, by that fact alone, distinguish an illuminating thought about consciousness from a familiar paragraph that says little. Gem Finder arose from the desire to search for what is extraordinary within and across subjects.

The first instrument deliberately separates several possible meanings of “interesting”:

| Axis | Intended use | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conversational surprise | Notice a departure from the preceding exchange. | A surprising reset or mistake may have little value. |
| Conceptual contribution | Find an added distinction, argument or reframing. | A plausible contribution can still be wrong. |
| Creative connection | Find a useful or unusual connection between ideas. | Association alone does not establish explanatory value. |
| Expressive force | Find language that communicates with unusual effect. | Beauty and fluency do not establish truth. |
| Conceptual complexity | Describe the relationships and structure developed. | Complexity is not a quality ranking. |
| Substance & specificity | Find articulated, inspectable detail. | Detailed unsupported claims can receive high scores. |
| Topic-profile distance | Find an uncommon mixture of broad-topic scores relative to other lineages in a frozen reference. | This is not worldwide originality or semantic truth. |
| Word count | Examine length and its relation to the other measures. | Length is an observed attribute, not a merit score. |

The first six are JEV ratings. Topic-profile distance is computed from existing broad-domain scores; word count is computed from the target text. The scatterplot matrix allows these measures to be compared without collapsing them into one universal ranking.

The development program assessed **1,000 messages/recorded replies**, producing **6,000 ratings** for **$0.090679344** in reported usage. One whitespace-only historical reply was retained and explicitly identified; a separate sensitivity analysis excludes it. It was not silently replaced.

The larger sample reinforced the researcher’s concern about length. In the 900-message expansion, word count correlated **0.694 with complexity**, **0.602 with contribution** and **0.625 with substance**, compared with **0.062 with surprise**. These associations cannot distinguish real elaboration from assessor preference or sample composition, but they make a single “best text” ranking questionable.

A particularly useful counterexample came from the initial pilot: a detailed but unsupported proposed consciousness test received a substance score of 99.75. This exposes a boundary in the instrument. Explicit detail is not epistemic validity. Calling the output a gem because its score is high would erase the very distinction the researcher needs to make.

The project has therefore built a **candidate-finding instrument**, not a validated automatic detector of intellectual value. Its usefulness must be established through what it helps a reader discover. See [Gem Finder](docs/GEM-FINDER.md) and [Gem expansion](docs/GEM-EXPANSION.md).

## 8. Giving the search a more recognizable intellectual character

The researcher found the initial Gem axes useful but too vague: they lacked a recognizable intellectual character. That led to a different question. Could JEV classify a message using features such as Borges-like, Plato-like or Spinoza-like thought? Could those sit beside discourse practices such as Reddit-like discussion or an X/Twitter-like post?

Mind & discourse now explores **16 thinker/writer lenses and 11 discourse practices**. The thinkers are Plato, Aristotle, Spinoza, Descartes, Hume, Kant, Hegel, Nietzsche, Wittgenstein, Heidegger, Borges, Kafka, Hemingway, Montaigne, Simone Weil and Nagarjuna.

The progression from names to definitions to conceptual portraits is significant. “Spinoza-like” can invoke a broad learned association. A procedural definition can make criteria inspectable but feel dry or incomplete. The researcher’s sketch—God as Nature, a single reality unfolding through its own necessity, a life striving to persist—suggested a more vivid account of what to recognize.

The resulting portraits are interpretive tools, not quotations or comprehensive accounts of a philosopher. They may help express what a reader is seeking, but they also change the construct being measured. The completed 60-text pilot compared names only, definitions only, names plus definitions, and names plus portraits: **240 requests and 6,480 probabilities**, for **$0.047085024**.

The differing results are evidence that operationalization matters. They do not show which wording is objectively correct. An author name should function as a declared search lens, not a claim of authorship, influence, imitation or philosophical achievement. Nor should a discourse-practice label be used as a hierarchy of cultural worth.

This development also clarified the researcher’s intended role: **evaluate the classifier as an instrument**, rather than become a full-time annotator of every snippet. A person can ask whether a lens makes useful distinctions, whether its top results are coherent, and what it misses. Selected examples support that judgment; they do not have to become an exhaustive personal training set. See [Conceptual portraits](docs/MIND-DISCOURSE-PORTRAITS.md) and [Pilot results](docs/MIND-DISCOURSE-RESULTS.md).

## 9. The needle in the haystack: a proposed discovery workflow

The components now suggest a workflow that goes beyond sorting by a single score. The complete integrated workflow below is a proposal; the lab already implements many of its parts.

First, describe the reading purpose. A scientific counterexample, a beautiful compact expression, an original hypothesis and a behaviorally unusual exchange are different kinds of discoveries. “Gem” should not conceal those differences.

Next, identify the relevant population and conditions. A message unusual for one model may be routine for another. A rare profile within a topic differs from a rare topic in the entire corpus. A highly scored sentence may inherit its significance from its partner’s earlier message.

Then use multiple assessments to create candidate queues. For example, one could look for short messages with strong expressive scores, philosophical messages with unusual topic mixtures, or an abrupt shift in a trajectory followed by sustained partner uptake. These are search recipes to evaluate, not assertions that the selected messages are valuable.

A good reading queue should also preserve diversity. If twenty near-duplicate messages from one lineage fill the top twenty positions, a ranking has wasted attention even if its scores are internally consistent. Candidate sampling across lineages, models, lengths and topics can expose more kinds of material.

Finally, return to the original message and its conversation. The reader decides whether the candidate is illuminating, merely polished, unclear, factually unsupported, or valuable for a reason the classifier did not capture. Save the finding with its source and a brief explanation. Disagreements are useful evidence for evaluating the instrument.

The quality of such a system can be investigated through **useful discoveries per unit of reading time**, the diversity of discoveries, and the kinds of important material it misses. A practical future comparison would present equal-sized, source-linked reading queues from a search recipe and from a random or simple baseline, hide the scores and queue origin where feasible, and ask which queue better served the declared reading purpose. Broader claims would need more than one reader and more than one purpose.

Precision among retrieved candidates can be studied with limited reading. Recall is harder: the system cannot prove it found all gems without examining what it did not retrieve. Random samples, low-score tails, deliberately short examples and weakly classified messages must remain part of evaluation.

A failed prediction, an ordinary exchange that resists an apparent attractor, or a counterexample to a favored theory can be a scientific gem. Discovery must not mean collecting only beautiful confirmations.

## 10. Why the economics matter—and what they do not settle

The recorded costs make repeated assessment practical at this project’s scale:

| Assessment | Recorded scope | Reported usage cost | Status |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Broad/specific topic corpus | 11,054 messages; 53 questions; 585,862 scores | $4.849454694 new usage | Complete; one overload reservation tracked separately. |
| Gem Finder, combined | 1,000 assessed records; 6,000 ratings | $0.090679344 | Complete development samples. |
| Four-wording Mind & discourse pilot | 48 real messages + 12 controls; 240 assessments; 6,480 probabilities | $0.047085024 | Complete. |
| Full Mind & discourse expansion | 18,309 / 19,316 assessments; 494,343 probabilities | $3.961531728 new usage | Stopped with missing assessments. |

These are historical measured costs for their specific inputs and pinned model, not current price quotations or forecasts for arbitrary workloads. They exclude researcher time, implementation effort and the cost of the coding assistant. They should not be added into a supposed all-in project cost; reuse and the broader project’s other calls require separate accounting.

This distinction became practical when the researcher noticed that supervising cheap JEV calls with an expensive conversational agent could dominate the cost. Routine execution moved into detached deterministic Python workers. Reasoning effort belongs in defining measures, interpreting results and resolving problems; a frozen, finite batch does not need a large model to supervise every request.

Low inference cost makes richer measurement possible. It does not make a measurement valid, remove the cost of reviewing it, or guarantee reliable service. Attention remains the scarce resource the proposed system should conserve.

## 12. What this milestone establishes

The laboratory now supports a connection that was absent at the beginning: a model conversation can be preserved as an experimental object, mapped through several explicit assessment schemes, visualized across time and across messages, and revisited as an exact source for a human finding.

Its strongest achievement is currently an instrument and a body of inspectable exploratory evidence. The recurring experiential topics are research observations worth testing further. The low cost of assessment is demonstrated for the recorded workloads. The usefulness of Gem Finder as a guide to valuable discoveries is promising but has not yet been independently demonstrated.

The central next scientific task is to evaluate the instruments: which questions distinguish worthwhile candidates, which reward verbosity or familiar forms, and which fail to notice compact or unconventional insights? In parallel, the next experimental task is to collect more balanced comparisons that can separate model tendencies from the conditions that elicit them.

Beyond this corpus, the same general approach could be explored for generated drafts, simulation logs, research brainstorming or other collections too large to read serially. Such transfers would need their own criteria, data permissions and evaluations. This project does not establish success in those settings.

The ambition is nevertheless clear. Cheap generation creates a surplus of text. Cheap, inspectable assessment may help turn that surplus into a usable field of inquiry. A useful laboratory preserves the route back from a score to a message, from a message to its context, and from a human discovery to the evidence that supports it.

**The gem is not the score. The score is a way of helping a person find it.**
